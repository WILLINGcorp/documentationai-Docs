## Purpose

The Schedule tab keeps project milestones, deadlines, and key dates close to the work so owners and collaborators can coordinate timing inside the Collab project.

Use it to:

- Define the detailed timeline that supports the high-level timing in the Overview.
- Give collaborators a shared view of upcoming milestones and deadlines.
- Track schedule changes as the project moves from planning into active work.
- Record the final timing of the project for future reference.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">

The Schedule appears as **Timeline** in the Production stage navigation. Keep it aligned with the high-level timing described in the [Overview](/overview), and use the Schedule for the detailed milestones and dates behind that plan.

</Callout>

## When to update it

Update the Schedule whenever a date, milestone, dependency, or delivery expectation changes.

Typical moments to update:

- **Before sending invites**: Add the initial milestones, key dates, and deadlines so invitees can understand the expected timing.
- **After the proposal is accepted**: Confirm the schedule with collaborators and adjust dates to reflect the agreed plan.
- **During active work**: Update progress and dates when work moves ahead of or behind the plan, then communicate changes through Project Chat.
- **Before each milestone**: Review the next dates, confirm what needs to happen, and flag potential delays early.
- **At completion or archive**: Record what actually happened so the Schedule remains a useful project record.

## What to include

Focus the Schedule on dates that help collaborators understand what needs to happen and when.

- **Milestones**
  The major points that mark progress through the project.
- **Key dates**
  Dates for planned work, reviews, decisions, and other events that affect collaborators.
- **Deadlines**
  The dates by which a deliverable, review, or decision needs to be completed.
- **Dependencies**
  Timing relationships where one milestone or activity needs to happen before another can move forward.
- **Current status**
  A clear indication of what is upcoming, in progress, delayed, or complete.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">

Use milestone names that describe an outcome, such as “Approve final storyboard” or “Deliver review assets.” Clear names help collaborators understand the action without opening another project tab.

</Callout>

## Best practices

Keep the Schedule useful as a shared working reference.

- **Start with the dates that matter most**: Add milestones and deadlines that affect decisions, handoffs, or collaborator availability before adding less important detail.
- **Keep dates consistent**: Use the same timing assumptions in the Schedule and the Overview so collaborators do not have to reconcile conflicting plans.
- **Update changes promptly**: Change the Schedule when timing shifts, then explain the change and its impact through Project Chat.
- **Review the next milestone first**: Keep upcoming work visible and use the schedule review to identify risks before they become missed deadlines.
- **Record the actual outcome**: At completion or archive, preserve the final timing instead of leaving only the original plan.
- **Separate timing from requirements**: Keep schedule dates in the Schedule and track verification, agreements, and release-related work in [Compliance](/compliance).

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">

If a schedule change affects agreements, releases, rights, or other compliance requirements, review [Compliance](/compliance) as well as the Schedule before treating the new date as final.

</Callout>

## Workflow: keep your Schedule useful

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up the timeline before or right after sending invites" icon="calendar" title-type="p">
    Define the project's main milestones, key dates, and deadlines. Add enough detail for invitees and collaborators to understand the expected sequence, while keeping the high-level timing aligned with the [Overview](/overview).

    Success looks like: someone opening the project can understand the major stages, important dates, and deadlines before deciding how to participate.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Share the timeline after the proposal is accepted" icon="calendar" title-type="p">
    Review the Schedule with collaborators once the proposal is accepted. Confirm that everyone can see the timeline, understand the milestone names, and identify the dates that affect their work.

    Success looks like: collaborators share the same understanding of the schedule and can identify what they need to complete and when.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Track progress during active work" icon="calendar" title-type="p">
    Update milestone status and dates as work progresses. When timing shifts, revise the Schedule and communicate the change, its reason, and its impact through Project Chat.

    Success looks like: the Schedule reflects the current plan, and collaborators know about changes before those changes affect their work.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and adjust before milestones" icon="calendar" title-type="p">
    Check upcoming dates before each major milestone. Confirm that required work is on track, identify possible delays, and adjust the timeline early when the current plan is no longer realistic.

    Success looks like: upcoming work has a current date, likely delays are visible early, and collaborators know which milestone needs attention.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Finalize the schedule at completion or archive" icon="calendar" title-type="p">
    Review the timeline when the project is complete or archived. Record the dates and milestones that actually occurred, including meaningful changes from the original plan.

    Success looks like: the Schedule provides a concise record of how the project progressed and when major work was completed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Role-specific guidance

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Project owner (sender)" icon="user">
    As the sender, you create the initial Schedule and maintain it as the project changes.

    - Set up the main milestones and deadlines before sending invites, or immediately after sending them if the proposal is still being refined.
    - Use the Schedule with the [Overview](/overview) to give invitees both the project context and the detailed timing.
    - Confirm the timeline after collaborators accept the proposal, especially when their availability or responsibilities affect dates.
    - Update the Schedule when work shifts and communicate the change through Project Chat.
    - Before completion or archive, record the actual timing so the project retains an accurate history.

    Success looks like collaborators can use the Schedule as the shared source for detailed timing throughout the project.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Collaborator (receiver)" icon="users">
    As a receiver, you use the Schedule to understand when your work is expected and how your timing connects to the project.

    - Review the timeline when you accept the proposal and identify milestones or deadlines that affect your contribution.
    - Ask the project owner to clarify dates that do not explain the expected handoff, review, or decision.
    - Check the Schedule before upcoming milestones and communicate timing risks through Project Chat.
    - Refer to the updated Schedule when planning work, rather than relying on an earlier version of the timeline.
    - Confirm that final dates reflect what happened before the project is completed or archived.

    Success looks like you can plan your contribution from the Schedule and recognize when a change affects your work.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common issues

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="The Schedule does not match the Overview" default-open="false">

  **Cause:** The detailed milestones or deadlines changed without updating the high-level timing.

  **Fix:**

  - Compare the Schedule with the timing in the [Overview](/overview).
  - Update the Schedule with the current detailed dates.
  - Update the Overview when the change affects the project's overall timing.
  - Tell collaborators about the change through Project Chat.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Collaborators are unsure which date to follow" default-open="false">

  **Cause:** A date changed, but the current Schedule or the related communication does not make the new plan clear.

  **Fix:**

  - Keep the current date in the Schedule.
  - Mark the affected milestone or deadline clearly.
  - Explain what changed and what collaborators need to do through Project Chat.
  - Remove or correct outdated timing where it could cause confusion.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="A milestone is at risk of delay" default-open="false">

  **Cause:** Work required for the milestone is not progressing according to the current timeline.

  **Fix:**

  - Review the milestone and its upcoming dependencies.
  - Adjust the date when the original timing is no longer realistic.
  - Flag the delay early through Project Chat.
  - Check [Compliance](/compliance) if the delay affects agreements, releases, rights, or other requirements.

  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
