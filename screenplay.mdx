## Purpose

The Screenplay tab keeps script or scene planning material available to the team throughout the Production stage of a Collab project.

Use it to:

- Structure the narrative into scenes with clear locations, characters, and descriptions.
- Add script pages or dialogue for each scene.
- Coordinate scene flow with the visual direction in [Moodboard](/moodboard).
- Keep scene timing aligned with the [Schedule](/schedule).

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">

Treat the Screenplay as the working reference for narrative structure and scene content. Cross-reference each scene with the Moodboard for visual style and the Schedule for timing.

</Callout>

## When to update it

Update the Screenplay whenever the narrative or scene plan changes.

Typical moments to update:

- **Before sending invites**: Add the initial scene structure and any script material collaborators need to understand the project.
- **While invites are pending**: Clarify scene descriptions, dialogue, and open narrative questions based on early feedback.
- **After acceptance**: Confirm the working scene order, key dialogue, and responsibilities with collaborators.
- **During execution**: Record revised, added, or removed scenes and verify that the Schedule still reflects the current plan.
- **Before production milestones**: Review the Screenplay with the Moodboard and Schedule so narrative, visual, and timing decisions remain aligned.

## What to include

- **Scene breakdown**
  A numbered list of scenes with locations, characters, and a one-line description of the action or purpose of each scene.

- **Script pages and dialogue**
  The script text associated with each scene, including clear scene headings and the dialogue or action needed for review.

- **Scene-to-schedule mapping**
  The timing, milestone, or production activity associated with each scene so collaborators can connect narrative flow to project timing.

- **Visual references**
  Links or references to the Moodboard material that establishes the visual style, setting, tone, or composition for each scene.

## Best practices

- **Keep scene descriptions concise**: Use one line to communicate the scene's purpose before collaborators read the full script material.
- **Use consistent scene headings**: Identify locations and scene context the same way throughout the Screenplay.
- **Preserve scene order**: Number scenes so collaborators can discuss changes precisely and identify where a scene belongs in the narrative.
- **Connect decisions across tabs**: Update the Moodboard when a scene's visual direction changes and update the Schedule when its timing or production needs change.
- **Make revisions visible**: When you add, cut, or substantially revise a scene, update the Screenplay and verify the related Schedule entry.

<Callout kind="tip">

Review one scene at a time across the Screenplay, Moodboard, and Schedule. This makes it easier to catch a mismatch between the narrative, visual direction, and timing before the team begins work.

</Callout>

## Workflow: keep your Screenplay useful

<Steps>
  <Step title="Establish the scene structure" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

    Create a numbered scene breakdown with the location, characters, and a one-line description for each scene. Put the scenes in the order the team should use when discussing the narrative.

    Success looks like: someone joining the project can understand the narrative structure and the purpose of each scene without reading the full script.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Add script pages or dialogue" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

    Upload or write the script text for each scene. Use clear scene headings so collaborators can match script material to the numbered scene breakdown.

    Success looks like: collaborators can find the dialogue or action for a specific scene and understand where that material belongs in the narrative.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Cross-reference visual and scheduling plans" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

    Review the [Moodboard](/moodboard) for visual style references and the [Schedule](/schedule) for timing. Confirm that each scene has the visual context and schedule information needed to support the planned work.

    Success looks like: the Screenplay, Moodboard, and Schedule describe the same scene flow with no conflicting locations, sequence, or timing.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Share with the team" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

    Notify collaborators that the Screenplay is ready for review. Walk through the scene structure, key dialogue, and any decisions that affect the Moodboard or Schedule.

    Success looks like: project owners and collaborators share the same understanding of the narrative, scene order, and review priorities.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Update as scenes evolve" icon="book-open" title-type="p">

    When you revise, add, or cut a scene, update its script material and scene number. Verify the related entries in the Schedule and the visual references in the Moodboard.

    Success looks like: the Screenplay reflects the current narrative and every affected scene remains aligned with its visual direction and scheduled work.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Role-specific guidance

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Project owner (sender)" icon="user">

    As the sender, you establish the initial narrative structure and maintain the Screenplay as the project changes.

    - Create the scene breakdown before sending invites so collaborators can understand the planned story and scene flow.
    - Add enough script material for collaborators to evaluate the work and prepare their contributions.
    - Coordinate Screenplay changes with the Moodboard and Schedule instead of updating one tab in isolation.
    - Walk collaborators through major scene decisions after they accept the proposal.

    Success looks like collaborators can review the narrative, understand their role in the work, and identify the current version of each scene.

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Collaborator (receiver)" icon="users">

    As a receiver, use the Screenplay to understand the narrative and connect your work to specific scenes.

    - Read the scene breakdown and relevant script material when you receive a project proposal.
    - Compare each scene with the Moodboard to understand the intended visual direction.
    - Check the Schedule for timing, milestones, and work connected to the scenes you support.
    - Raise questions when a scene change affects visual references, timing, or another collaborator's contribution.

    Success looks like you can contribute to the assigned scenes without relying on separate, outdated copies of the script or scene plan.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Common issues

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="The scene order does not match the Schedule" default-open="false">

    **Cause:** Scenes were added, removed, or reordered in the Screenplay without updating the related schedule entries.

    **Fix:**

    - Compare the numbered scene breakdown with the scene-to-schedule mapping.
    - Update the [Schedule](/schedule) to reflect the current scene order and timing.
    - Tell collaborators which scenes changed so they review the correct sequence.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="A scene has script material but no visual reference" default-open="false">

    **Cause:** Script or dialogue was added without linking the scene to the visual direction in the Moodboard.

    **Fix:**

    - Open the [Moodboard](/moodboard) and identify the visual references for the scene.
    - Add or update the scene's visual reference so collaborators can connect the narrative to the intended style.
    - Review the scene again with the Screenplay and Moodboard side by side.

  </Expandable>

  <Expandable title="Collaborators are reviewing different scene versions" default-open="false">

    **Cause:** A revised, added, or cut scene was not communicated after the Screenplay changed.

    **Fix:**

    - Update the current scene structure and script material in the Screenplay.
    - Notify collaborators about the change and identify the affected scene numbers.
    - Verify that the Moodboard and Schedule use the same current scene plan.

  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
