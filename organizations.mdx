## What an organization is in ModelBoard

An organization in ModelBoard represents a business, studio, or enterprise entity that coordinates work across multiple people and profiles in the adult entertainment industry.

An organization has its own profile, settings, and features, and then links team members to that profile as sub-accounts. Owners control team access through requests, approvals, and a Capability Matrix for staff actions.

<Callout kind="info">

ModelBoard is the operating system and professional networking platform for the adult entertainment industry. Organizations sit at the center of that experience for teams and companies.

</Callout>

## Organization profiles vs sub-accounts

Organization structures in ModelBoard are built from two pieces: a primary organization profile and linked sub-accounts for each team member.

### Organization profiles

Organization profiles represent the business entity itself:

- A **Business**, **Studio**, or **Enterprise** profile
- Owns the brand identity, settings, and organization-level features
- Acts as the "parent" that other profiles connect to

The organization profile is what you see when you operate as the company: configuring studio pages, managing talent, handling licensing apps, and so on.

### Sub-accounts (team member accounts)

Sub-accounts represent people who work within an organization:

- Created as separate profiles linked to the organization
- Have a **role** of owner or staff
- Log in as themselves but act **on behalf of** the organization when switched into that context
- Receive staff access from default capabilities plus any owner-managed overrides

This design keeps each person's identity and access separate, while still letting them act inside one or more organizations. Owners can review requests, grant staff capabilities, and apply changes without sharing credentials.

<Callout kind="tip">

Use separate sub-accounts for each person in your team. Do not share logins. This keeps permissions clear and makes audits accurate.

</Callout>

## Roles and the Capability Matrix

ModelBoard has two organization-level roles: **Owner** and **Staff**. Owners always have full access, while staff access starts from a default capability set that owners can adjust.

Use the **Capability Matrix** on your organization team or settings page to review what each role can do.

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Owner" icon="shield">

Owners control the organization identity, team access, and sensitive settings. Each organization has at least one owner.

Owners can:

- Approve, reject, and invite sub-accounts
- Review incoming and outgoing transfer requests
- Toggle staff capabilities in the Capability Matrix
- Reset staff capabilities back to the default set
- Save or discard unsaved capability changes

Owners always retain full access, even when they turn staff capabilities off.

<Callout kind="alert">

Assign owner only to people you fully trust with your organization's identity, relationships, billing, compliance posture, and sensitive configuration.

</Callout>

</Tab>

<Tab title="Staff" icon="users">

Staff accounts are team members who work inside the organization without full ownership control.

By default, staff can perform day-to-day actions such as posting studio news, creating casting forms, uploading to organization storage, managing collaboration projects, and sending organization messages.

Owners can grant or remove additional staff capabilities, including approval-related access, from the Capability Matrix. When an owner saves changes, the update applies immediately to every staff sub-account in the organization.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

### Default staff capabilities

Staff access starts with a focused set of operational capabilities. Owners should treat these defaults as a starting point, then adjust them for the organization's workflow and risk level.

Default staff capabilities include:

- **Post studio news**
- **Create casting forms**
- **Upload to organization storage**
- **Manage collaboration projects**
- **Send organization messages**

Staff do not receive sensitive capabilities by default, including editing the organization profile, managing billing, approving sub-accounts, managing BioLink, or signing compliance as the organization.

<Callout kind="tip">

Review the Capability Matrix after onboarding new staff, changing responsibilities, or completing a sensitive project. Capability changes apply immediately to all staff in the organization.

</Callout>

## How organization membership works

Membership in an organization is request-based and must be reviewed. Owners manage requests from **Sub-Account Requests**, which separates legacy invitations, new join requests, and transfers into dedicated tabs.

### Sub-Account Requests tabs

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Invites" icon="mail">

The **Invites** tab shows pending legacy invitations that still need to be sent.

Use **Send** to create the invited sub-account and send the invitee a password setup email. After the invitation is sent, the invitee can finish access setup from their email instead of sharing credentials with the organization.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Join" icon="user-plus">

The **Join** tab shows new sub-account requests from people who want to join the organization.

Owners review each request and choose **Approve** or **Reject**. Approving adds the person to the organization; rejecting closes the request without granting access.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Incoming" icon="arrow-right-left">

The **Incoming** tab shows transfer requests where another organization or sub-account is asking to move into your organization.

Approve only when you recognize the person or profile, understand why the transfer is happening, and are ready for the organization to become responsible for that access.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Outgoing" icon="arrow-right-left">

The **Outgoing** tab shows transfer requests where a sub-account linked to your organization is moving away from it.

Use this tab to approve or reject the release side of a transfer. Approved transfers update the sub-account's organization connection after the required approvals are complete.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

### Join and invite flow overview

<Steps>

<Step title="Create or identify the organization" icon="building2">

If you are setting up the team, start by creating or claiming the **Business**, **Studio**, or **Enterprise** organization profile that represents your company.

If your organization profile already exists, confirm who the current **Owner** is. They will handle invitations, requests, and capability settings.

</Step>

<Step title="Review the request queue" icon="users">

Open **Sub-Account Requests** and check the tab that matches the request type: **Invites**, **Join**, **Incoming**, or **Outgoing**.

The pending count helps owners see how many requests still need attention.

</Step>

<Step title="Approve, reject, or send" icon="check-circle">

Choose the action that matches the request:

- **Send**: sends a pending legacy invitation email.
- **Approve**: grants the requested membership or transfer approval.
- **Reject**: closes the request and can include a reason.

</Step>

<Step title="Confirm active access" icon="zap">

After approval, the member sees the organization in their account switcher and can operate in that context according to their role and staff capabilities.

After a capability change, staff should refresh or revisit the affected area if access does not update immediately in their current session.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout kind="info">

All membership changes flow through explicit requests, invitations, and approvals. There are no public invite links that allow unknown users to join your organization.

</Callout>

## Transfers between organizations

Sometimes a profile needs to move from one organization to another (for example, when a studio changes management or an agency restructures).

Transfers follow an approval-based flow similar to membership:

- A **transfer request** is created to move a sub-account from one organization to another.
- The current organization reviews the request from **Outgoing**.
- The destination organization reviews the request from **Incoming**.
- Approved transfers update the sub-account's organization connection and are recorded for auditability.

Transfers help you keep historical continuity while still reflecting changes in how your business is structured.

<Callout kind="info">

Ownership and role changes are audited behind the scenes, so you retain a trail of who controlled an organization and when.

</Callout>

## Switching between accounts and organizations

Many people on ModelBoard wear multiple hats: creator, studio staff, business owner, or enterprise manager. The **account switcher** lets you move between these roles without multiple logins.

### When you see the account switcher

You see the account switcher when your user has access to more than one profile, for example:

- Your personal creator or professional profile
- One or more Business or Studio organizations as owner or staff
- One or more Enterprise organizations, possibly linked to multiple studios

### How to switch between accounts

<Steps>

<Step title="1. Open the account switcher" icon="swap-horizontal">

From the main navigation, open the account switcher. You will see a list of profiles and organizations your user can act as.

Each entry represents a distinct context (for example, "Jane as Creator", "Sunset Studios", "Aurora Talent Agency").

</Step>

<Step title="2. Choose the profile or organization" icon="cursor-click">

Select the profile or organization you want to switch into.

ModelBoard updates your active context to that selection. Navigation, features, and data now reflect that account.

</Step>

<Step title="3. Confirm you are in the right context" icon="eye">

Check the header or context indicator to confirm you are acting as the intended profile or organization.

Before performing sensitive actions (like approvals or configuration changes), always verify you are in the correct organization.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout kind="tip">

Treat the account switcher as the "hat" you are wearing. Make sure you are wearing the right hat before approving requests or changing settings.

</Callout>

## Enterprise organizations and studio links

Enterprise organizations often manage multiple studios, brands, or subsidiaries. To support this, ModelBoard lets Enterprise accounts link directly to Studio organizations.

Conceptually, this works as:

- An **Enterprise** profile sits above one or more **Studio** profiles.
- Enterprise owners can switch into linked studios through the same account switcher flow.
- Linked studios retain their own identity while still rolling up to the enterprise for coordination and oversight.

This structure is useful for:

- Agencies managing multiple production studios
- Holding companies coordinating several brands
- Platforms with separate but related studio entities

## Under the hood (for technical readers)

If you work with integrations or need to design processes around ModelBoard's structure, it helps to understand how organizations are modeled internally.

<Callout kind="info">

This section describes concepts at a high level and is not an API reference. For concrete endpoints and schemas, refer to the API Reference.

</Callout>

- **Organization profiles** are special profiles whose type is business, studio, or enterprise.
- **Team members** are sub-accounts linked via an internal organization identifier.
- **Roles** are captured as owner or staff at the sub-account level; there is no third admin role.
- **Staff capabilities** are resolved from default staff access plus owner-managed organization overrides.
- **Membership changes** (invitations, joins, approvals, rejections, transfers) are handled through dedicated request objects and backend workflows.
- **Enterprise-to-studio relationships** are modeled as links that allow switching from the enterprise context into a specific studio context.

This model keeps every action tied to a specific profile and role, which supports clear auditing and reliable permission checks.

## Security and access control

Security for organizations is based on **individual logins, request approvals, and owner-managed capabilities**, not shared credentials or open links.

Key principles:

- Each person uses their own login and becomes a **sub-account** of the organization.
- **Owners** must approve new members and incoming or outgoing transfers.
- **Owners** always have full access and should be assigned sparingly.
- **Staff** receives default operational capabilities unless owners change them in the Capability Matrix.
- **Approval access** can be granted to staff only when an owner enables the relevant capability.
- There are **no** public invite URLs that allow unknown users to join.
- Sensitive actions and ownership changes are recorded for auditability.

<Callout kind="alert">

Never share your personal login with team members. Instead, add them as staff or owners through the join-and-approve flow so their actions are tracked correctly.

</Callout>
