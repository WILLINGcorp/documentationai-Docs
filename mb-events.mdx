## Overview

MB Events helps teams publish events, manage attendance, and review participation without exposing more attendee information than each role needs.

Use event pages to share event details, collect participation signals, and keep organizers informed. Attendance views are role-based: attendees can review their own records, organizers can review attendance for events they manage, and public views should show aggregate counts instead of individual attendee lists.

<Callout kind="info">

Attendance is privacy-sensitive. Do not use public event pages to expose individual attendees unless the event workflow explicitly supports that disclosure and the attendee has agreed to it.

</Callout>

## Who can see attendance

Attendance information appears differently depending on the viewer's relationship to the event.

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Attendees" icon="users">

Attendees can see their own attendance records so they can confirm where they registered, joined, or participated.

This view is personal to the attendee. It helps them track their own event history without giving them access to another attendee's participation details.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Organizers" icon="shield">

Organizers can review attendance for the events they manage.

Use organizer views to check participation, coordinate follow-up, and understand event performance. Organizer access should stay limited to the events the organizer owns or administers.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Public viewers" icon="eye">

Public event views should rely on aggregate counts, such as total attendance or participation totals.

Avoid showing names, profiles, contact details, or individual attendance records in public views. Aggregate counts give visitors useful context without turning attendance into a public directory.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

## Privacy-safe event publishing

Before publishing or promoting an event, review how attendance information appears to each audience.

<Steps>

<Step title="Check the attendee experience" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">

Confirm attendees can find their own event status or attendance history after they sign in.

Success: an attendee can verify their own participation without seeing another attendee's private attendance details.

</Step>

<Step title="Review organizer access" icon="shield" title-type="p">

Make sure the organizing team can access attendance records for the events they manage.

Success: organizers can review the records they need for coordination and follow-up, but they do not receive broad access to unrelated events.

</Step>

<Step title="Use aggregate counts publicly" icon="bar-chart" title-type="p">

For public event pages, show totals or summary counts instead of individual attendee information.

Success: public viewers understand event activity from aggregate numbers, while attendee identities and attendance records stay protected.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What to avoid

Treat attendance data like private account activity unless the event has a clear reason to make participation public.

- Do not publish individual attendee names, profiles, or contact details on public event pages by default.
- Do not let attendees browse other attendees' attendance records.
- Do not use screenshots or exports of attendance lists in public marketing materials without the right permission.
- Do not give organizers access to attendance for events they do not manage.

<Callout kind="tip">

When you need to communicate event momentum, use aggregate language such as total registrations, confirmed attendees, or checked-in participants.

</Callout>

## Troubleshooting attendance visibility

<ExpandableGroup>

<Expandable title="An attendee cannot find their event record" default-open="true">

Ask the attendee to sign in with the same account they used for the event. Attendance records are tied to the attendee's own account view, so switching accounts can make a record appear missing.

If the record still does not appear, have the organizer review the event attendance list from the organizer view.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="An organizer cannot review attendance">

Confirm the organizer is assigned to the event they are trying to manage. Organizer attendance access is tied to the events they own or administer.

If the organizer recently joined the team, refresh their event permissions before asking them to check again.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="A public page shows too much attendee detail">

Replace individual attendee details with aggregate counts. Public pages should communicate event activity without exposing who attended or how each person participated.

Review any linked assets, screenshots, or embedded materials as well, because those can expose attendee details even when the page itself uses aggregate counts.

</Expandable>

</ExpandableGroup>
