## Track event attendance without exposing attendee lists

MB Events helps organizers manage event schedules, registrations, check-ins, and session attendance while keeping individual attendee activity limited to the people who need it.

Attendance data is sensitive because it can reveal where someone planned to be, which sessions they joined, and who was involved in an event. ModelBoard shows individual session attendance to the attendee and event organizers, while public and broader views should use aggregate headcounts instead of raw attendee records.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">

Public event pages may show totals such as registered attendees or session capacity. They should not be treated as attendee directories.

</Callout>

## Who can see session attendance

Session attendance uses a practical privacy model: people can see the details needed for their role, and everyone else sees summary information.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Attendees" icon="user">

Attendees can see their own event and session activity, including the sessions they registered for or attended when that information is available in their account.

They should not expect to see the full list of people attending a session unless the event organizer has provided a separate, approved networking or directory experience.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Event organizers" icon="shield">

Event organizers can review individual attendance records for the events they manage. This supports check-in, capacity planning, session operations, follow-up, and compliance review.

Organizer views may include attendee search, registration management, attendance dashboards, check-in status, and session-level reporting depending on the event setup and organizer permissions.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Public viewers" icon="eye">

Public viewers should see aggregate event information, such as how many people registered or whether a session is approaching capacity.

Public or broader views should rely on headcounts and capacity indicators, not raw attendee records or individual session attendance lists.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## What public views should show

Public event pages are designed for discovery and registration decisions. They can show useful event context without revealing private attendee activity.

Public or broadly shared views may include:

- Event title, description, date, location, and publication status.
- Session names, times, speakers, and room or location details.
- Aggregate registration or attendance totals.
- Capacity information, such as current registrations compared with maximum attendees.

Public or broadly shared views should not include:

- Raw attendee records.
- A session-by-session list of individual attendees.
- Check-in history tied to named people.
- Private registration details, notes, or organizer-only status fields.

<Callout kind="alert" collapsed="false">

Do not export or repost individual session attendance from organizer tools into public channels. Use aggregate headcounts when sharing event performance, capacity, or turnout outside the organizer team.

</Callout>

## Organizer workflow

Use organizer tools when you need to act on individual attendance records. Use aggregate reporting when you are sharing updates beyond the people responsible for running the event.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review event registrations" icon="users" title-type="p">

Open the event management area for the event you organize, then review the registration list or attendee dashboard. Confirm that you are working in the correct event before taking action on attendee records.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Manage check-in and session attendance" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">

Use the check-in or attendance tools during the event to confirm who attended. Individual records are intended for organizer operations, such as resolving access questions, managing room capacity, and following up with attendees.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Share summaries instead of records" icon="bar-chart" title-type="p">

When reporting event results to a broader audience, share totals such as registrations, check-ins, session attendance counts, or remaining capacity. Avoid sharing names, account details, or raw attendance exports unless your organization has approved the disclosure.

  </Step>
</Steps>

A successful organizer workflow leaves attendees checked in correctly, session counts updated for operational reporting, and individual records visible only in organizer or attendee-specific contexts.

## Privacy and compliance reminders

Treat event attendance as personal activity data. Even when attendance is not legally restricted in your region, your organization may still have contractual, privacy, or safety obligations around how attendee information is stored and shared.

Use these guidelines when handling attendance data:

- **Limit access** to organizers and team members who need attendee records to run the event.
- **Use totals for reporting** when sharing outcomes with sponsors, partners, public audiences, or larger internal teams.
- **Review exports before sharing** to make sure names, account identifiers, check-in timestamps, or session-level records are not included unintentionally.
- **Coordinate with compliance owners** before using attendance data for credentialing, legal review, or regulated record-keeping.

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">

If your event uses networking, directories, or attendee introductions, make sure that experience has its own consent and visibility rules. Session attendance alone should not be used as a public attendee directory.

</Callout>

## Troubleshooting visibility questions

<ExpandableGroup>
  <Expandable title="Why can I only see a headcount on a public event page?" default-open="false">

Public event pages show aggregate information so people can understand event interest or capacity without exposing individual attendee activity. If you organize the event, use the organizer tools to review attendee-level records.

  </Expandable>
  <Expandable title="Why can an attendee see their own session activity but not everyone else in the session?" default-open="false">

Attendees need access to their own schedule and attendance history, but they do not automatically need access to other attendees' session records. Broader visibility should come from an approved networking or directory feature, not from raw attendance data.

  </Expandable>
  <Expandable title="What should I share after an event?" default-open="false">

Share aggregate results such as total registrations, total check-ins, session attendance counts, and capacity trends. Share individual attendee records only with approved organizers or compliance stakeholders who need that information.

  </Expandable>
</ExpandableGroup>
